Think about the spaces your central system has never handled well: a garage workshop that bakes by July, an addition the ductwork never reached, a bonus room that runs too warm or too cold, or an older Sherman home built without ducts in the first place. Those are exactly the jobs a ductless mini split was made for. Nothing travels through leaky ductwork, because each wall-mounted head is fed by a line set that passes through a small hole in the wall. Inside the outdoor unit, an inverter compressor modulates to match the load, so the room holds within a fraction of a degree, quietly and on very little power. And when winter comes, it works as a true cold-climate heat pump.

Design is where mini split projects are won or lost, and Sherman HVAC does it deliberately. Each zone gets sized to its actual load, because a head too big for the room cools fast but leaves the air clammy. Multi-zone systems bring their own engineering: the outdoor unit's capacity has to be matched sensibly across the connected heads, line set lengths and elevation changes must stay inside the manufacturer's tables, and the choice between wall-mounted, ceiling-cassette, and floor units affects both comfort and how the room looks. Our experienced technicians walk the space with you, place heads where the physics and the sightlines agree, and route line sets cleanly, in finished line-hide outdoors.

On installation day, the small steps that customers never see are the ones we refuse to skip. Every flare is formed carefully, torqued to spec, and leak-checked, because a leaking flare joint is the number one way mini splits fail. The line set gets a nitrogen pressure test, then a deep vacuum confirmed on a micron gauge, before refrigerant ever moves. We set up condensate to drain by gravity or through a reliable pump, trim the charge for the actual run length, and test each zone in heating and cooling. Why Professional Ductless Mini Split Installation Matters

Since a mini split looks like a simple box on a wall, a lot of them get installed like one, and they fail in predictable ways. The weakest link is the flare joint. Form one badly or leave it under-torqued and it seeps refrigerant slowly, so nobody notices until the head is blowing room-temperature air months later. Skip the nitrogen test and the measured vacuum, and moisture stays in a circuit that inverter compressors do not forgive. Push the line set past the manufacturer's length or lift limits and you lose capacity without a single alarm. Forget the drain slope and the first muggy week leaves a stain down the wall.

Sizing is tighter with ductless than with central air. The smallest heads on the market are already more than many bedrooms need, so picking one size too big means short cycles, sticky humidity, and a room that never settles. That is why our experienced technicians size every zone on its real load, then back it up with careful install work: each flare torqued and leak-tested, the circuit pressure-tested and evacuated to a measured vacuum, charge adjusted for the line length, and every head commissioned in heating and cooling. Handling refrigerant is federally regulated, and the unit needs a correctly sized circuit and disconnect. Done this way, a mini split runs quiet and efficient for fifteen years with nothing to report.

How to Prevent Ductless Mini Split Installation Problems

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Schedule service once a year so charge, flares, and drainage get verified before small issues grow
  • Winter defrost pauses are expected; heavy ice buildup on the outdoor unit is your cue to call
  • Glance at the condensate drain outlet in summer to confirm it is actually dripping
  • Keep the outdoor unit clear of snow, leaves, and grass clippings in every season
  • Rinse or wash the indoor unit's filter screens monthly; they load up faster than furnace filters
  • Do not wait for a musty smell; get the blower wheel and coil cleaned by a pro on a regular basis
  • Choose a setpoint and leave it alone, since inverters run best without constant on and off switching
